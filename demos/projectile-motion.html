Ignoring air resistance, which launch angle sends the ball farthest? Is there another angle that lands at the same spot as 30°?

Show the answer

45° gives the maximum range. Angles that add up to 90° (30° and 60°) land at the same spot.

  • The horizontal velocity never changes during the flight (the amber line is flat in the velocity graph).
  • The vertical velocity falls at a steady rate $g$, is zero at the top, then points downward.
  • Kinetic and potential energy trade places, but their sum stays constant. Change the mass: the path stays the same, only the energies scale.
Common mistake. At the top of the path the acceleration is not zero. Only the vertical velocity is zero; the acceleration is still $g$, pointing down.

Horizontal and vertical motion are independent. With launch speed $v_0$, angle $\theta$ and gravity $g$:

$$x(t)=v_0\cos\theta\,t,\qquad y(t)=v_0\sin\theta\,t-\tfrac12 g t^2,\qquad R=\frac{v_0^2\sin 2\theta}{g}$$

Since $\sin 2\theta$ is largest at $\theta=45^\circ$, that angle gives the longest range on level ground.
