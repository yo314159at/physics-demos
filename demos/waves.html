Two identical waves travel toward each other along a string. What does the string look like when they overlap? Are there points that never move?

Show the answer

A standing wave. The pattern stops travelling and the string oscillates in place. Points called nodes, spaced half a wavelength apart, never move; between them are antinodes with twice the single-wave amplitude.

  • The dots (small pieces of the string) only move up and down. The pattern travels, not the material.
  • The snapshot graph $\psi(x)$ and the time graph $\psi(x_0,t)$ have the same shape, one against position and the other against time. The wave speed links their scales.
  • Where the snapshot is steepest the dot moves fastest. For a single wave, the string-velocity graph is the slope graph times $-v$.
  • Choose Standing and drag probe $x_0$: it snaps to quarter-wavelengths. At odd multiples it stays at zero (a node).
Common mistake. The string does not travel along with the wave. Each point oscillates around its own position; only the shape moves.

A sinusoidal wave moving right with amplitude $A$, wavenumber $k=2\pi/\lambda$ and angular frequency $\omega=2\pi f$:

$$\psi(x,t)=A\cos(kx-\omega t+\varphi),\qquad v=\frac{\omega}{k}=\lambda f$$

In a medium where the speed does not depend on frequency, waves add (superposition). Two equal waves in opposite directions give a standing wave, and two nearby frequencies give beats:

$$\psi=2A\cos(kx)\cos(\omega t),\qquad f_\text{beat}=|f_1-f_2|$$

Both waves share one medium, so the wavelength follows from the speed and the frequency: $\lambda=v/f$. That is why dragging the wavelength bracket changes the frequency.

  2. Choose Beats and read the beat period from the $\psi(x_0,t)$ graph.
  3. Drag the wavelength handle to double the frequency at fixed wave speed. What happens to the wavelength?
