If you double the mass, does the block oscillate faster or slower? What if you double the spring stiffness? Does the starting stretch change the period?

Show the answer

Doubling $m$ slows it down (period × $\sqrt2$). Doubling $k$ speeds it up (period ÷ $\sqrt2$). The starting stretch does not change the period.

  • Kinetic and spring energy trade places, but with no damping their sum stays constant.
  • The phase portrait (velocity against position) is an ellipse without damping and a spiral with damping.
  • With damping the total energy decays and the swings shrink, yet the period hardly changes.
Common mistake. A bigger starting stretch does not make each swing take longer: the force is larger and the distance is longer, and the two effects cancel.

The spring pulls back with force $F=-kx$. Newton's second law, with a damping force $-bv$, gives:

$$m\ddot x=-kx-b\dot x,\qquad T=2\pi\sqrt{\frac{m}{k}}\ (b=0),\qquad E=\tfrac12 m v^2+\tfrac12 k x^2$$

The period depends only on $m$ and $k$, not on the amplitude. The simulation integrates the equation numerically with a fourth-order Runge–Kutta method, so the energy does not drift.

  1. Set damping to None, $m=1$ kg, $k=25$ N/m. Read the period, then predict it for $m=4$ kg before changing it.
  2. Switch to Heavy damping. Does the block still oscillate?
  3. Open the Forces graph and find the moments when the spring force is zero. What is the velocity there?
  4. Grab the block while it is moving. What does the phase portrait do?
